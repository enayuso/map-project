# How Google/Apple/HERE/TomTom/Waze Actually Source Data, and What Open Alternatives Exist for Belize

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Purpose:** [07-naver-kakao-proprietary-gis.md](07-naver-kakao-proprietary-gis.md) asked "how do Naver/Kakao really build their maps" and found the answer was *government-scale data infrastructure that doesn't exist in Belize, on top of the same class of open-source tooling this project already uses*. This follow-up asks the same question of the other major commercial map platforms — Google, Apple, HERE, TomTom, Waze — specifically for a small, thin-data country like Belize, and separately surveys the open/free geospatial datasets that could supplement this project's own OSM+PostGIS pipeline. All findings below are from live web research conducted 2026-09-10 (four parallel research passes plus direct spot-checks), not recalled from training data — citations are inline throughout.

## The headline finding

**None of the five commercial platforms surveyed have meaningful first-party ground-truth coverage of Belize.** Every one of them is, for Belize specifically, doing some mix of the same three things this project already does: **licensing/using OpenStreetMap, buying satellite/aerial imagery, and leaning on crowdsourced edits.** The difference between them and a hobby-budget Belize app isn't a fundamentally different data supply — it's decades of accumulated investment, a bigger crowdsourcing user base, and (for Naver/Kakao specifically, per doc 07) a domestic government data pipeline that has no Belizean equivalent. Concretely:

| Platform | First-party survey coverage of Belize? | What actually covers Belize on that platform |
|---|---|---|
| Google Maps | **No** — no Street View road coverage at all ([confirmed](#2-google-maps)) | Satellite/aerial imagery (Airbus/Maxar/Landsat mix) + undisclosed third-party/government content partners + crowdsourced edits |
| Apple Maps | **No** — Belize absent from Apple's own current ground-survey country list ([confirmed](#3-apple-maps)) | TomTom-licensed base data + OpenStreetMap (Apple both consumes *and* has run organized editing campaigns covering Belize) |
| HERE | Own survey fleet exists but Belize is explicitly rated only **"Intermediate"** tier, not HERE's best ([confirmed](#4-here-technologies)) | Blend of probe data, satellite imagery, and presumably licensed/OSM-adjacent sources at that lower tier |
| TomTom | Own survey fleet (MoMa) — **the one platform with a positive, official Belize coverage claim** ("detailed data," their top tier) ([confirmed](#5-tomtom)) | Own fleet + 600M-device probe network + community/government input, per TomTom's own multi-source model |
| Waze | Fully crowdsourced by design — no "first-party survey" concept applies | Belize's own Waze community wiki/forum looks largely dormant (a single unanswered forum post since Sept 2024) ([confirmed](#6-waze)) |

This project's OSM+PostGIS+Martin+Nominatim+Typesense+Valhalla stack is not a lesser-tier compromise relative to these platforms for Belize specifically — it's built on the same underlying data source (OSM) that Apple explicitly falls back to and that even Google and HERE plausibly draw on, without any of their proprietary polish obscuring what's actually there.

## 1. Methodology note

This document combines four parallel live-research passes (one per platform grouping below) with direct spot-checks I ran myself (HOT OpenStreetMap Team's actual Belize activity, Belize government data sources beyond BNSDI, and Copernicus's emergency-response mapping). Confidence is marked inline where a finding rests on inference rather than a directly-confirmed primary source, matching the practice established in [07-naver-kakao-proprietary-gis.md](07-naver-kakao-proprietary-gis.md). Several sub-findings could not be confirmed despite real effort — these are stated as open gaps, not papered over.

## 2. Google Maps

**Ground Truth, in history and in its current (unclear) status.** Google launched "Ground Truth" in 2008 to move off licensed vendor data (originally a TeleAtlas/NAVTEQ/ESRI mix) toward in-house-verified maps combining authoritative government data, Google's own Street View/satellite imagery run through computer vision, and manual review ([TechCrunch 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/09/03/googles-ground-truth-initiative-for-building-more-accurate-maps-now-covers-50-countries/), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/guides/ground-truth/)). It scaled to 40 countries by 2012 and 50 by 2014 ([TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/12/05/google-updates-maps-for-10-countries-and-regions-in-europe-ground-truth-now-at-work-in-40-countries)); no source after ~2015 uses the term as an active named program, and Google's own 20th-anniversary materials (Jan 2025) describe the pipeline generically — "Street View cameras... satellite and aerial images, and information from more than 1,000 third-party sources, such as local governments and users" — without naming Ground Truth at all ([CNBC via search snippet](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/18/google-maps-turns-20-adds-ai-features-new-countries-to-beat-apple.html)). Read this as "Ground Truth became how Google Maps normally works" rather than a discontinued program — an inference, not a confirmed fact.

**No Belize Street View exists.** Confirmed from three independent angles: Wikipedia's Street View coverage-history article never mentions Belize ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Street_View_coverage)); a travel blog states plainly that "Belize does not have full Google Street View coverage because Google's camera cars haven't driven the roads yet" ([Belize With Alvin](https://belizewithalvin.com/magellan-gps-to-google-maps-belize/)); and Google's own Street View Insights product page lists only US/Canada/Romania/Ireland/UK/Japan as supported — Belize absent ([Google Developers](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/street-view-insights/coverage)). As of August 2025, only Guatemala, Panama, and (newly) Costa Rica had official car-driven Street View anywhere in Central America. The one Belize-specific Street View asset that *does* exist is underwater imagery of the Belize Barrier Reef, captured via the **Catlin Seaview Survey** — a scientific/conservation partnership using diver-mounted rigs, unrelated to Google's normal survey program ([Google Street View Treks: Oceans](https://www.google.com/maps/about/behind-the-scenes/streetview/treks/oceans/)) — plus scattered user-submitted 360° photospheres at individual tourist sites via Google's open "Street View ready" contributor tool.

**Crowdsourcing history has a directly-relevant precedent.** Google Map Maker (2008–2017) was built specifically to fill in road/business data for regions with thin official data — exactly Belize's situation — before being folded into Local Guides/in-app edit suggestions in 2017 after repeated spam/vandalism problems ([TechCrunch shutdown announcement](https://techcrunch.com/2016/11/08/google-to-shut-down-map-maker-its-crowdsourced-map-editing-tool/), [2015 hack](https://techcrunch.com/2015/05/11/google-shuts-down-map-maker-following-hacks/)). In January 2012, Google partnered with the **World Bank** to open Map Maker's underlying data to governments/UN agencies specifically for disaster response and public-service mapping in developing countries — a near-exact precedent for what this project is trying to do at a smaller scale, though Belize was not one of the named 2012 pilot countries (Kenya, South Sudan, Tanzania, Sierra Leone, Ghana, Zambia, Nigeria, DRC, Moldova, Mozambique, Nepal, Haiti) ([World Bank press release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2012/01/16/world-bank-google-announce-map-maker-collaboration)). Today's Local Guides program (150M+ contributors globally per Google's own count) is the living successor.

**Who actually supplies Belize's Google Maps data? Undisclosed.** Google's Content Partners program lets governments/nonprofits submit authoritative data for free, but publishes no public roster of participants ([Content Partners help center](https://support.google.com/mapcontentpartners/answer/11929191?hl=en)). A live spot-check of Google Maps' own attribution strip over the Belize/Yucatán border area (performed during this research) showed "Map data ©2026 Google, INEGI" — but INEGI is Mexico's mapping agency, and the attribution overlay lists all providers visible anywhere in the current viewport, so this **cannot** be read as confirmation that INEGI data extends into Belizean territory specifically. No evidence of a Google–OpenStreetMap or Google–HOT data partnership (for Belize or anywhere) was found either — a real gap, not a confirmed absence. One useful, independently-sourced Belizean quote: a BNSDI official told a local outlet BNSDI is "Google Maps, just for Belize... [but] more accurate than what Google Earth has" ([7NewsBelize](https://7newsbelize.com/sstory.php?nid=50136)) — a Belizean government source explicitly benchmarking itself against Google, not evidence of a partnership.

## 3. Apple Maps

**Confirmed: zero first-party survey coverage of Belize, or of any Central American/Caribbean country.** Apple publishes its own current, authoritative list of every country under active ground survey (vehicle/pedestrian-backpack/Look Around/iPhone-collection) — as of this research, the only Latin American entries are **Brazil and Mexico** ([Apple Maps Image Collection locations](https://maps.apple.com/imagecollection/locations)). This is a direct primary-source confirmation, not an inference.

**Current sourcing model is a hybrid**, mixing Apple's own rebuilt survey data (in ~58 countries/regions as of the 2018–2025 rollout) with licensed/open data elsewhere. Apple's own attribution pages name **TomTom** and **OpenStreetMap** explicitly as base-map providers, plus Maxar for satellite imagery and a long list of POI aggregators (Factual, Foursquare, Yelp, TripAdvisor, Zomato, Booking.com) ([Apple Maps legal page](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/maps/legal-en.html), [full acknowledgements](https://gspe21-ssl.ls.apple.com/html/attribution.html)). HERE was not found named as a current Apple provider anywhere.

**Apple's OSM relationship is confirmed and two-way** — unusually well-documented compared to Google's opacity. Apple has used OSM "since iOS 6... in regions where commercial data providers have less extensive coverage" ([OSM Wiki: Apple](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Apple)), and separately runs an internal "Apple Data Team" (hashtag `#adt`) that builds OSM quality-analysis tooling (Atlas/Atlas Checks), funds outside contractors to work MapRoulette tasks, and has released building-footprint data back to OSM for France and Denmark. **Most directly relevant: Apple's own organized-editing activity log explicitly names Belize, Central America, the Caribbean, and Mexico** among regions its data team has worked on, with a stated practice of contacting the local OSM community first ([OSM Wiki: Organised Editing/Apple](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Organised_Editing/Activities/Apple)) — the single strongest piece of evidence found in this whole research effort that a major commercial platform has directly touched Belize's map data. One asymmetry worth noting: Apple's own Terms of Use forbid using Apple Maps/Look Around imagery as a source *for* OSM edits, even though Apple freely edits OSM itself.

**No dedicated Belize quality reporting exists**, but the closest analogous case — a smaller market outside Apple's survey program — is instructive. A 2026 Euronews investigation into missing villages on Apple Maps in Lebanon quotes experts attributing gaps to "limited data collection and a lack of investment... rather than deliberate removal," since Apple prioritizes "markets such as Europe and North America, where usage is stronger," and notes Lebanon's dataset remains in **"beta" status** ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/04/17/did-apple-remove-lebanons-villages-from-its-maps-it-says-they-were-never-there)). Belize is likely in a similar position, by analogy only.

**Belize businesses do have self-service access to Apple's business-listing tool.** Apple's current unified "Apple Business" platform (successor to Apple Business Connect) explicitly lists Belize under its Latin America/Caribbean region as having "Brand and Location Management" access — i.e., Belizean businesses can claim and edit their own Apple Maps place cards for free, the same as Google Business Profile ([Apple Business feature availability](https://support.apple.com/guide/business/feature-availability-axmef1c47twq/web)).

## 4. HERE Technologies

HERE blends its own ~300-vehicle survey fleet (2.2M km/year, LiDAR-equipped) with a "HD Live Map" that self-heals via probe/sensor data from 80,000+ connected sources, plus satellite imagery and a Mapillary street-imagery partnership ([Autocar India](https://www.autocarindia.com/auto-features/making-maps-here-and-now-412931), [HERE HD Live Map](https://www.here.com/platform/hd-live-map)). **HERE's own official coverage documentation places Belize in its middle "Intermediate Map" tier** — not the top "HERE Maps" tier, described as having "the richest level of attribution and highest level of coverage and quality" ([HERE coverage docs](https://docs.here.com/map-rendering/docs/coverage-info-map-image-api)). HERE does offer a no-credit-card freemium developer tier, but current limits are genuinely unclear — sources conflict between 250,000/month (a 2025-dated review) and as low as 30,000/month for geocoding (a 2026-dated pricing aggregator); this project's research could not resolve the discrepancy from HERE's own JS-rendered pricing pages. Not recommended as a real data source for this project given the tier classification and the licensing uncertainty, but worth knowing it exists as a benchmark.

## 5. TomTom

**The standout finding among the "big three" commercial platforms: TomTom's own official market-coverage documentation lists Belize under "detailed data" — TomTom's top coverage tier**, not the lesser "simplified data" tier ([docs.tomtom.com market coverage](https://docs.tomtom.com/map-display-api/documentation/product-information/market-coverage)). TomTom sources this via its own MoMa survey vans (20+ years running) plus sensor-derived observations from 600M+ connected devices, cross-checked against historical map data ([TomTom Newsroom](https://www.tomtom.com/newsroom/behind-the-map/super-sources-power-tomtoms-new-map/)). TomTom also has the most generous, clearly-documented no-card-required free tier of the three — e.g. 200,000 map tiles/month, 20,000 routing/geocoding requests/month per TomTom's current live pricing docs ([docs.tomtom.com/pricing](https://docs.tomtom.com/pricing)) — though third-party sources describe an older, differently-structured daily-limit version of this tier, so the exact current terms should be verified directly at signup before relying on it, and this project's research did not directly confirm TomTom's own Terms & Conditions language on commercial-use permissions. **Given the Locked Decision 3 hobby-budget/self-hosted preference, TomTom isn't something to switch to now** — but it's worth remembering as the one commercial fallback with a credible, official, positive Belize-coverage claim, should this project ever need a second opinion on road geometry or a paid upgrade path post-pilot.

## 6. Waze

Waze is fully crowdsourced (Google-owned since 2013, [$1.1B](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2013-06-09/google-is-said-to-be-acquiring-waze-for-1-1-billion)) and has **no general-purpose data/routing API for third-party developers at all** — its only sanctioned data-sharing channel, "Waze for Cities" (formerly the Connected Citizens Program), is a free, two-way exchange exclusively between Waze and government transportation/emergency-management agencies, not something a private app can join ([Waze for Cities](https://www.waze.com/wazeforcities/)). Costa Rica's government joined this program as early as 2014 ([Tico Times](https://ticotimes.net/2014/10/28/why-waze-is-so-incredibly-popular-in-costa-rica)) — a real precedent showing a comparably small Central American country was accepted — but no evidence was found of Belize's government participating, and it's unclear whether a partner government could even legally relay Waze's feed onward to a private third-party app. **Belize's own crowdsourced Waze map looks largely dormant**: its community-maintained Wazeopedia wiki is explicitly marked "a work in progress" with borrowed Canada/US content, and the Belize Map Update coordination forum has had exactly one post since it was created (September 23, 2024), with zero replies ([Belize Wazeopedia](https://www.waze.com/wiki/Belize/Main_Page), [Waze Discuss](https://www.waze.com/discuss/c/editors/belize/belize-map-update/4942)). Not a usable data source for this project by any path.

## 7. Open/supplementary geospatial data sources worth evaluating

Beyond the five commercial platforms, live research surfaced a real landscape of open datasets. Verdicts below are calibrated against Locked Decisions 3 and 4 (hobby/self-funded budget, must permit commercial redistribution).

| Source | License | Commercial-OK? | Belize evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Overture Maps Foundation** — Places/Addresses theme | CDLA-Permissive-2.0 (no share-alike, minimal attribution) | Yes | **Confirmed directly** — a real Belize City POI ("Eats and Sweets," bakery, sourced from Meta, dated Aug 2026) and a structured address with postcode were inspected live in Overture's own explorer tool | **Adopt** as a supplementary POI/address enrichment source — genuinely more permissive than raw OSM extraction, and includes Meta-sourced business data with postcodes OSM often lacks for Belize |
| **Overture Maps** — Buildings/Transportation/Divisions themes | ODbL (same as raw OSM) | Yes, with same share-alike obligation | Confirmed live (dense Belize City building footprints, neighborhood-level divisions down to "St. Martin de Porres") | Adopt only as a pre-cleaned convenience layer — does **not** change this project's existing OSM licensing posture at all |
| **Meta Data for Good** — High-Resolution Population Density | CC BY | Yes | Confirmed — dedicated Belize dataset on HDX with age/sex breakdowns | Adopt for population-weighted features (search ranking, service-area analysis); note it's a frozen ~2020 snapshot, not live |
| **Microsoft Global ML Building Footprints** | CDLA-Permissive-2.0 | Yes | **Not confirmed** — could not verify a Belize row in the dataset index in this research pass | Needs a follow-up check before relying on it |
| **Google Open Buildings v3** | CC BY 4.0 | Yes | Confirmed — Belize explicitly named in coverage claims | A safer substitute for the same "ML building footprint" use case than the unconfirmed Microsoft dataset |
| **Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX)** | Varies per dataset (no platform-wide license) | Mostly yes | Confirmed active Belize country page (boundaries, health facilities, population) | Adopt as a discovery/aggregator layer, not a source in itself |
| **GADM** (admin boundaries) | **Non-commercial only** — "redistribution or commercial use is not allowed without prior permission" | **No** | Confirmed present but shallow (country + 6 districts only) | **Do not adopt** without a paid license — and OSM's own admin boundary relations (already in this project's data, per [06-pilot-region-data-audit.md](06-pilot-region-data-audit.md)) are likely sufficient anyway |
| **geoBoundaries** (admin boundaries) | CC BY 4.0 | Yes | Confirmed — ADM0 through ADM2 for Belize, deeper than GADM's free tier | The correct free replacement for GADM, if this project ever needs boundary polygons beyond what OSM's own admin relations provide |
| **Kontur Population/Boundaries** | CC BY 4.0 | Yes | Confirmed dedicated Belize dataset | Worth considering alongside geoBoundaries |

| **Copernicus EMS Rapid Mapping (EMSR726)** | Free-to-use per Copernicus's general open-data policy; exact commercial-reuse terms for this specific product line not independently re-verified | Likely, unconfirmed | Confirmed, found independently: a real, dated activation for the same 2024 Belize wildfires (23 May–13 Jun 2024, Cayo + Toledo districts, ~65,182 ha, 10 map products) via [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/map/belize/belize-wildfires-extent-wild-fires-copernicus-emsr726-activation-cayo-district-11-jun-2024) and [MapAction](https://maps.mapaction.org/dataset/571c47f0-14cc-4e6b-a208-d4eb9411443c) | A genuinely interesting future overlay — see recommendations below |
| **Copernicus Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL)** | Free for preparedness/emergency/recovery use per program's own framing | Unconfirmed for general commercial use | Not independently verified for Belize tiles | A lead only, not a finding — needs its own follow-up if pursued |

## 8. Belize-specific findings beyond BNSDI (self-directed research)

These came from checking Belize's own institutional and community mapping activity directly, rather than the commercial-platform comparison above:

- **The Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT) has real, dated, concrete Belize activity already baked into the OSM extract this project uses.** A [2024 wildfire response activation](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Humanitarian_OSM_Team/Open_Mapping_Hub_-_Latin_America_and_The_Caribbean/Activations/2024_Belize_Wildfires_Response) ran three completed Tasking Manager projects (#16914, #16920, #16929) mapping buildings across Cayo and Toledo Districts between June 4 and August 19, 2024, tracing Esri World Imagery under the hashtag `#belizefires24` — this data is already live in OpenStreetMap, meaning it's already in this project's Geofabrik extract. Separately, a February 2025 **Emergency Resources Mapping pilot** — HOT + CDEMA + Belize's NEMO + EliteCSoft + Taiwan's International Cooperation and Development Fund — trained 30 youth volunteers across Belize City, Belmopan, San Ignacio, Santa Elena, and Corozal to map hazards, flood-prone zones, and communication towers ([HOT update](https://www.hotosm.org/updates/strengthening-disaster-preparedness-in-belize-through-youth-led-open-mapping-and-open-data-collection/)) — it's unclear whether this pilot's output was published directly into OSM or kept as a separate stakeholder deliverable, worth checking if pursuing this further. **Actionable**: this project's own [06-pilot-region-data-audit.md](06-pilot-region-data-audit.md) only audited Belize City and Ambergris Caye — extending that same audit methodology to Cayo/Toledo would likely show a real, HOT-driven building-data quality bump worth confirming empirically rather than assuming.
- **The Statistical Institute of Belize (SIB) has its own GIS unit, independent of BNSDI/the Ministry of Natural Resources** — it maintains a geo-referenced dwelling frame with building-level coordinates and photos collected for the 2022 Census ([SIB GIS unit](https://sib.org.bz/about-us/gis/)). Its own page states a public Geoserver/WMS to share this data is still "a future endeavor," not live today. This is a second, previously-unidentified government contact worth a similarly polite outreach email once (or in parallel with) a BNSDI/Ministry response, since it's a genuinely distinct data-collection effort from a different agency.
- **Belize's Land Information Centre (LIC) is not an independent access path** — `portal.bnsdi.gov.bz/lic` confirms LIC is itself hosted as a sub-section of the same BNSDI portal already confirmed down.
- **HDX's "Belize healthsites" dataset is not new data** — it traces back to [healthsites.io](https://healthsites.io/), the Global Healthsites Mapping Project, which is itself ODbL-licensed and built from OpenStreetMap. It's a convenient pre-filtered export of data this project's own `planet_osm_point` extract already contains, not an independent source — worth knowing so it isn't mistaken for something additive.
- **Belize has no general government open-data portal at all** — a Center for Data Innovation survey groups Belize with Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua as Latin American countries lacking one ([Center for Data Innovation](https://datainnovation.org/2017/07/the-state-of-open-data-portals-in-latin-america/)). BNSDI is genuinely the only structured attempt at one, which is useful context for why its outage (tracked since the original check in [05-belize-official-data-verification.md](05-belize-official-data-verification.md)) matters more than it might for a country with alternate portals. **Re-confirmed still down as of today** (`portal.bnsdi.gov.bz` → connection refused, live-checked during this research).

## 9. What this means for this project

Restating the doc's headline finding as concrete next steps, ranked by actual effort-to-value:

1. **No architecture change is warranted.** Every major commercial platform surveyed is, for a country Belize's size, doing some combination of "license/consume OSM, buy satellite imagery, lean on crowdsourcing" — the exact three legs this project already stands on. This isn't a budget-constrained compromise; it's the same strategy at a smaller, self-hosted scale.
2. **Consider Overture Maps' Places/Addresses theme as a low-effort POI/address enrichment pass** alongside the existing OSM-based Typesense index — its CDLA-Permissive-2.0 license is more permissive than OSM's ODbL, and it demonstrably includes Meta-sourced Belize business data with postcodes.
3. **A Copernicus EMS-style hazard overlay is a genuinely interesting, cheap future feature**, not just a research curiosity — EMSR726's wildfire-extent data (already free, and covering exactly the same event HOT's building-mapping activation responded to) could become an optional overlay layer on top of the existing satellite/hybrid basemap toggle, if wildfire/disaster context is ever a product priority. Not scoped as a phase item yet — flagging the idea, not committing to it.
4. **Extend the [06-pilot-region-data-audit.md](06-pilot-region-data-audit.md) methodology to Cayo/Toledo** to empirically check whether HOT's 2024 building-mapping activation actually shows up as better data quality there, before assuming it did.
5. **A second, low-key outreach email to the Statistical Institute of Belize's GIS unit** is worth considering once there's a BNSDI/Ministry response (or in parallel) — it's a distinct government data-collection effort, not just the same portal under another name.
6. **GADM should be avoided entirely** given its non-commercial-only free tier; if administrative boundary polygons are ever needed beyond what OSM's own boundary relations already provide, geoBoundaries (CC BY 4.0) is the correct free substitute.
7. **TomTom is worth remembering, not adopting** — the one commercial platform with an official, positive ("detailed data") Belize coverage claim, and a real free tier, should this project ever need a second opinion on road geometry or outgrow the free/self-hosted stack post-pilot (matching the same "revisit once budget allows" framing [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) already applies to Maxar/Airbus/Planet imagery).
