# 13 — Sourcing Belize's Seasonal Highlights (2026-09-14)

Follow-up to live research into recent Kakao/Naver Map features: Kakao's seasonal "cherry-blossom map" (Mar 2026) — a temporary curated layer over ~100 named cherry-blossom spots, each with a simple 3-stage status (pre-bloom / starting / full bloom) and a predicted date range — has no Belize analog for the content itself, but the *mechanism* (a lightweight curated-JSON seasonal-status layer, same shape already used for `sharing/data/ferry_routes.json`/`dive_sites.json`/`border_crossings.json`) maps directly onto real, regulated or scientifically-documented Belize seasons. Researched three candidates before building anything, same discipline as doc 12 — checked against primary sources, not assumed.

**Headline: lobster's closed season is the strongest-sourced item here (one official regulator page, consistently corroborated) — the other two are real and well-documented but don't reduce to one clean official date range the way lobster does.** Built into `sharing/data/seasonal_highlights.json` with each entry's confidence level carried through to the app itself (see `mobile/README.md`'s "Seasonal highlights" section), not smoothed over.

## Spiny lobster (and queen conch) — Belize Fisheries Department

- **Spiny lobster (Panulirus argus) closed season: March 1 – June 30, every year.** Open season: July 1 – end of February. Source: **Belize Fisheries Department's own regulations page**, https://fisheries.gov.bz/regulations/ — states verbatim "Closed season: 1st March to 30th June." Corroborated by multiple current-year (2026) news reports of the season closing on schedule (San Pedro Sun, Breaking Belize News, Love FM — all Feb 2026, describing traps being pulled by Feb 28 ahead of the March 1 closure). No disagreement found across sources; enforced under the Fisheries Resources Act (Cap. 210), harmonized regionally under OSPESCA regulation OSP-02-09.
- **Queen conch (Lobatus gigas): nominally July 1 – September 30 closed, but quota-driven — closes early whenever Belize's national catch quota is reached, which has happened every recent season checked** (2025-26 season closed early on Apr 6, 2026; the 2024 season closed even earlier, Jun 9, 2024). Same official regs page states this explicitly: "Closed season: 1st July to 30th September **or when catch QUOTA is met**." **This means the nominal date range is an outer bound, not a reliable predictor of the actual closure** — shown in the app with an explicit note about this, not presented as a hard date.

## Whale shark aggregation — Gladden Spit and Silk Cayes Marine Reserve

- **Season: roughly March–June, peaking April–May**, tied to the lunar cycle around each full moon — whale sharks gather to feed on spawning Cubera snapper (Lutjanus cyanopterus), not on a fixed calendar window.
- **Two different day-counts around the full moon, from two credible but different kinds of source, that don't precisely agree:**
  - Tourism-operator framing (what actually governs when dive trips run): **3 days before the full moon to 3 days after the last-quarter moon**, sightings following the April/May/June full moons specifically. Source: Splash Dive Center (licensed Placencia operator), https://splashbelize.com/scuba-diving-sites/whale-sharks-at-gladden-spit/.
  - Peer-reviewed science (the underlying mechanism): Heyman et al., *Journal of Fish Biology* (2005) — aggregations observed 2 days before to 12 days after full moon, March–September, peak abundance April–July, spawning itself most frequently observed in May, in a ~50-minute daily window around sunset.
- **Shown in the app as a broad month window (mid-March–mid-June) rather than picking one of the two day-counts as authoritative** — the discrepancy is real, not a research gap I could resolve further without primary access to the paywalled paper.
- **Location**: 16.567°N, 88.033°W (16°34′N 88°02′W), Gulf of Honduras, ~36km/90min by boat from Placencia. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladden_Spit_and_Silk_Cayes_Marine_Reserve, corroborated by the reserve's own official page at https://fisheries.gov.bz/gsscmr/ (Belize Fisheries Department holds legal authority; day-to-day co-management is by the Southern Environmental Association, seabelize.bz).

## Sea turtle nesting

- **No single official national date range exists** — unlike lobster, Belize has no one regulator page covering this; each managing body publishes its own site's season.
- **Belize Audubon Society**, for Half Moon Caye Natural Monument (Lighthouse Reef Atoll): loggerhead and green turtles nest **June–November**; broader marine-turtle nesting-and-hatching activity commences in May and usually ends in November, with peak hatchling emergence Sept–Oct. Source: https://belizeaudubon.org/hmcnm/, figures corroborated via https://www.dive-the-world.com/posts/lighthouse/belize-half-moon-caye-seasonal-marine-life.php (explicitly attributed there to Belize Audubon Society).
- **SEA Belize** (Southern Environmental Association), for the reserves it co-manages in southern Belize: active nest **monitoring June–September** — narrower, likely reflecting their active monitoring window rather than the full biological nesting season. Source: https://seabelize.bz/services/sea-turtle-nesting/.
- **Used here: May–November as the outer/composite range, with peak activity flagged as June–July** — an honest composite, not a single official figure, documented as such in the data file's own `confidence` field.
- **Species confirmed nesting in Belize**: Hawksbill, Loggerhead, Green (SEA Belize, Belize Audubon Society).
- **Named nesting sites with real sourcing** (used for map markers): Gales Point/Manatee Bar (Hawksbill — one of the most important sites in the western Caribbean, ECOMAR), Half Moon Caye Natural Monument (all three species, Belize Audubon Society, 17°12′17″N 87°32′11″W), South Water Caye (Hawksbill hotspot, Hawksbill Sea Turtle Recovery Program monitoring, 16°49′9″N 88°5′1″W). Port Honduras Marine Reserve, Ambergris Caye (Robles/Rocky Point beaches), and Turneffe Atoll all have real turtle-nesting activity documented too, but without site-level coordinates I could independently confirm to the same standard — left out of the map-marker set for now rather than guessing a point.

## Design consequence for the app

Given lobster is reliably one fixed annual date range while whale sharks and turtles are genuinely fuzzier, `seasonal_highlights.json` doesn't force all three into the same precision — each entry carries its own `confidence` string, shown in the panel, rather than presenting a researched-but-uncertain window with the same authority as an official regulator's fixed date. See `mobile/src/seasonal/seasonalStatus.ts` for the shared active/upcoming/off-season computation every entry (regardless of confidence level) is run through the same way.
